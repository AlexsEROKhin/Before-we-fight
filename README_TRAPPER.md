# Trapper encounters

Open `Assets/Scenes/Arena.unity`, then expand `Enemy Camps`. All ten squads contain an editable `Trapper - Squad XX` prefab instance.

Each Trapper replaces the right-hand RingCreep at the same local position. The original RingCreep object is retained but **inactive** in each group. To restore the old group, disable the Trapper and re-enable that RingCreep in the Unity hierarchy. No squad or enemy was deleted.

The Trapper has two hits of health and uses the existing sword hit dispatch, hit feedback, death cleanup, enemy health bar, Io healing adapter and minimap marker. It maintains distance, stops while casting, locks the player's current world position, checks that position is within the tiled arena ground and free of static obstacles, then shows a stationary 0.7-second warning ring. The amber stain remains on the ground until the arena scene ends. While the hero is inside it, normal movement is slowed by 25% and the stain deals 1 HP every second, starting one second after entry. The slow continues for 3 seconds **after leaving the stain**. Dash speed and invulnerability are unchanged. The Trapper can cast again after its cooldown, but will not place a new stain over an active one. Overlapping slows use the strongest effect rather than multiplying speed loss. Killing the Trapper during the warning removes that warning; already-active stains remain. Damage per tick, tick interval and exit-slow duration are editable on `Assets/Prefabs/StickyPuddle.prefab`; cast timing and slow strength are editable on `Assets/Prefabs/Trapper.prefab`.

After a non-lethal hit the Trapper interrupts its cast, turns nearly transparent for four seconds and retreats from the hero. During this escape it cannot cast or be struck by the sword; its health blocks and minimap marker are hidden. Io immediately drops the tether, cannot select or heal the invisible Trapper, and accumulated partial healing is discarded. Existing resin stains remain. The duration, eight-second reactivation cooldown, visibility and retreat speed are editable in the `Invisibility after a non-lethal hit` section of `Assets/Prefabs/Trapper.prefab`.

## Art and editing

The Trapper has 21 transparent, point-filtered 32x32 frames in `Assets/Art/Imported/Enemies/Trapper/`: four idle, six walk, five cast, two hit and four death. They are connected to the editable `Assets/Prefabs/Trapper.prefab`. The five distinct pose anchors were generated from the approved simple creep concept, then reduced to a nine-colour palette. Additional in-between frames use pixel-aligned offsets, mirroring or a pouch/eye colour change; some poses deliberately hold for more than one frame. No elaborate armour or effects were added. `Assets/Art/Source/Trapper/Trapper.aseprite` contains all 21 editable frames with five named animation tags. The original pose sheet is beside it as `trapper_pose_sheet.png`. `Tools/BuildTrapperFrames.ps1` reproduces the 32x32 PNGs from the pose sheet and `Tools/BuildTrapperAseprite.lua` assembles the native Aseprite document. Both build scripts overwrite their output; do not rerun them after hand-editing without first copying your edited art. After manual edits in Aseprite, export the changed frames to the corresponding PNG names to update Unity without changing GUIDs.

`StickyPuddle` now uses `sticky_warning_v2.png` (hollow ring) and `sticky_puddle_v2.png` (filled resin) with a warning pulse, short appearance expansion and fade-out. The larger generated source images are in `Assets/Art/Source/Trapper/`; `Tools/BuildTrapperCircle.ps1` exports them to 32x32 sprites. The old `sticky_resin.png` remains in the project as an unused backup. The previous yellow square came from the export script accidentally recolouring transparent pixels on two frames (`trapper_idle_2.png`, `trapper_cast_3.png`); their alpha and the export script are fixed. The first, overly detailed Trapper concept was rejected and is not in the project.

## Verification

The runtime C# scripts compile against the installed Unity 6.2 assemblies with `Tools/CompileCheck.csproj` (zero errors). All 21 character sprites have transparent borders. Scene YAML has unique file IDs, ten Trapper prefab instances and ten inactive replacement RingCreeps. A Unity Play Mode pass is still required for movement, trigger overlap, dash, visual scale and balance. The batch Editor still exits with code 1 during licensing before scene import on this machine, so no successful live gameplay test is claimed.
